## What actually counts as an "LLM gateway"

Three different things get called this, and conflating them is the most common mistake:

1. **Multi-provider proxy/router** — one OpenAI-compatible endpoint in front of many vendors (Anthropic, OpenAI, Bedrock, Vertex). Adds virtual keys, budgets, rate limits, fallbacks, cost attribution. *This is what most people mean.*
2. **API-gateway-with-AI-plugins** — your existing Kong/Envoy/APISIX data plane, extended. Same L7 infrastructure, LLM-aware plugins.
3. **Inference router for self-hosted model fleets** — KV-cache-aware routing, prefill/decode disaggregation, vLLM replica scheduling. Solves a completely different problem (GPU efficiency, not vendor abstraction).

If you buy a #1 expecting #3 behaviour, you'll be disappointed, and vice versa.

## What actually bites in production

These are the things that decide the outcome, and they're mostly absent from feature matrices:

- **Provider feature lag is the #1 problem.** Gateways normalise to an OpenAI-chat-completions-shaped schema. Anthropic's Messages API, OpenAI's Responses API, and their respective newer capabilities don't map cleanly onto it. Extended thinking, tool-use streaming semantics, citations, structured outputs — the gateway is always weeks-to-months behind the provider. Check whether your candidate offers a **raw passthrough mode** that skips translation. Many teams end up running the gateway in passthrough for their primary provider and using translation only for fallbacks.
- **Prompt caching breaks silently through gateways.** Anthropic's cache breakpoints are position-sensitive: if the gateway reorders, re-serialises, or injects into the request, you get cache misses and a large cost increase with no error. Test this explicitly by watching cache-read vs cache-creation token counts before and after inserting the gateway.
- **Spend-logging write amplification.** LiteLLM-style per-request spend rows into Postgres become the bottleneck well before the proxy CPU does. Plan for batching, or shipping logs to a columnar store instead.
- **Token counting is estimated, not exact**, for any provider whose tokeniser isn't public. Budget enforcement built on gateway-side estimates will drift from your actual invoice. Reconcile against provider billing, don't trust the dashboard.
- **You just created a SPOF on your critical path.** Every request now depends on the gateway, its Postgres, and its Redis. Ask what happens when Redis is unavailable — fail open or fail closed? The answer differs by product and is often configurable but badly defaulted.
- **Semantic caching is mostly a trap.** It demos beautifully and produces subtly wrong answers in production, because embedding similarity is not semantic equivalence for prompts that differ in a number or a negation. Exact-match caching is boring and works.
